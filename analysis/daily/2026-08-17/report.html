Part 2 — Full-history replay methodology
83,977 historical moments, not 38 — and a real bug found and fixed mid-scan

The bot already has the right infrastructure for this (built for analysis/v3_condition_analysis_2026-08-13): signals.checked_market_states records the exact indicator snapshot (regime/RSI/ADX/EMA/momentum at M5/M15/M30/H1) every time any strategy evaluated the market — 83,977 such moments across the bot's whole history, not just the handful of times one strategy happened to fire. Filtering those down to moments matching dd_ranging_breakout's exact live condition shape (H1=ranging, M5=breakout, EMA bull, RSI≥50) gives 982 instances — 26× the live sample — each forward-simulated against real M1 candle highs/lows to a 4-hour horizon.

Bug caught and fixed: "H1 freshness" initially looked 100% degenerate Caught before it became a false finding

Extended the scan to bucket by how many minutes had elapsed since the referenced H1 candle's bar_time — testing whether staler H1 labels predict worse outcomes. First run: all 982 moments landed in one bucket. The bucket boundaries only went up to "45-60min," with an unbounded catch-all default — real gaps ran from 65 minutes up to 3,075 minutes (an outlier), average 101.5 minutes. H1 staleness is a genuine structural fact (the strategy can never see the live, still-forming hour — see the code comment below), but it doesn't predict win vs. loss.

Part 3 — Does the M15 pattern generalize?
Tested on dd_oversold_bounce — the pattern inverts completely

Before applying "skip if M15 disagrees" anywhere else, tested it against dd_oversold_bounce — the only other strategy that, like dd_ranging_breakout, never checks M15 in its own entry gate. Same method, its exact condition shape (H1=trending_down, M5=trending_down|breakout, EMA bear, RSI<50, ADX≥25), 245 matched historical moments:

Why it flips

dd_ranging_breakout is a trend-continuation BUY — M15 also trending down means the medium-term chart disagrees with the trade, which is bad. dd_oversold_bounce is a BUY betting on a bounce within an existing downtrend — M15 trending down there means the downtrend context is clean and confirmed, exactly what that setup wants. Same indicator, opposite meaning, because the two strategies have opposite relationships between their trade direction and the trend they're reading. A blanket rule would have helped one and actively hurt the other.

Part 5 — Decision and fix (implemented and shipped)
M15-trending-down filter added to dd_ranging_breakout only

Only dd_ranging_breakout shipped a change — it's the only strategy where the evidence was clean, well-powered, and pointed one direction. Honest before/after, computed from real win/loss counts at TP=1.5×ATR / SL=1.0×ATR:

NWin rateTotal (ATR-units)Avg/trade
Baseline (no filter)98263.0%575.50.586
After filter91963.9%558.50.608
Excluded (M15 trending_down)6350.8%17.00.270

Not a free win: the excluded 63 trades were still net-positive on their own (+17.0 ATR-units), just weaker than the rest. The filter trades a small amount of total cumulative profit for meaningfully higher win rate and per-trade quality (fewer, cleaner trades) — a real tradeoff, not a pure upside, and worth knowing which one you're optimizing for.

if m15.Regime == config.TrendingDown {
