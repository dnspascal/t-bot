Part 4 — Market-state pipeline
A SELL fired with zero H1 confirmation, straight into a strong H1 uptrend — because H1 was silently missing from the data every strategy reads

Investigating the balance report above led to a second, unrelated bug — a real position that should never have opened the way it did:

MetricValue

Max favorable excursion$0.54 into profit — never got further
Net-$8.40 (sl_hit)
H1 regime at entrytrending_up, ADX 40.36 — directly against the SELL
Root cause — H1 was genuinely absent, not just stale

Checked checked_market_states — the exact snapshot recorded per-signal at decision time — across the restart window. The "H1" key was completely missing from every signal between 13:10 and 13:55, reappearing exactly at 14:00, the next real H1 candle close. Traced to the actual mechanism: Warmer.WarmupAllTimeframes correctly seeds each timeframe's indicator calculator from historical candles at startup — but nothing ever copies that into b.marketStates, the live map strategies actually read. That map only gains a period's entry when that period's next live candle closes, so after every restart, H1 (or M30) is invisible to every strategy for up to a full bar's duration (up to 59 minutes for H1) — even though the underlying data was already correct.

breakout's H1 gate is written as "if H1 data is available, check it" rather than "require it, hold if missing" — so it fired completely blind to H1, straight against a strong uptrend.

Not a one-off — quantified across every historical occurrence Root-caused with real data before shipping
Closed tradesWin rateNet

13 occurrences since 2026-07-27, every one lining up with a restart. Three other strategies — session_momentum, sr_bounce, session_open — share the same soft-fail-open pattern and are exposed the same way.

Decision — implemented and shipped

Bot.SeedMarketStates(), called once after warmup and before streaming starts, hydrates the live map immediately from the already-warmed processors — fixes all four affected strategies at the root. Verified live: on the deploy's own restart, H1 was present for all 10 strategies on the very first evaluation cycle (previously took up to 59 minutes).

Part 6 — trend_follow breakout regime, stress-tested
Three real losses in one day on the regime just shipped in Part 2 — four different fixes proposed, backtested, and rejected
"i think same thing happened on first trade is about to happen again these trades are now in deep losses"

A live BUY at 16:10 hit SL 34 minutes later (-$19.86), entered right as a fast rally (~$33 in 20 minutes) topped out. The trade's own "Market States at Entry" table (Part 5, put to immediate use) showed the real picture: only M5 said trending_up outright — M15/M30/H1/H4 all said breakout, and critically, H4's own EMA had already crossed bearish (4397.32 < 4411.22) while D1 was explicitly trending_down. M5 and M15 RSI were 76.1 and 79.0 — both overbought. trend_follow reads none of H4, D1, or RSI at all.

Two more BUYs (18:25, 18:30) fired later the same day, same H1 breakout regime, same pattern — both eventually closed via time_stop=stalled_60m at -$11.18 and -$13.58. Three for three, all from the regime handling shipped that morning.

Four candidates, tested in isolation, all rejected
CandidateResult
RSI-extreme filter — hold if M5/M15 RSI overbought/oversold against the tradeBackwards. At every threshold tested, the "exhausted" trades won more, not less (e.g. thr=70: kept 22.4% WR/-$734.64 vs excluded 43.9% WR/+$958.73). Makes sense for a trend-follower: sustained RSI extremes are a strong-trend signature, not exhaustion.
H4/D1-opposition filter — hold if H4 or D1 EMA opposes the directionH4 alone: excluded bucket actually did better (43.0% WR, net positive) — would cut winners. D1 alone: aggregate looked mildly positive (kept +$454 vs excluded -$230) but flipped sign repeatedly week to week with no explanation across all 9 weeks — noise, not a real edge.
Spike-chase / velocity filter — hold if price moved >1-3x ATR in the 15-45min before entryAlso backwards, and just as clean as the RSI result: chasing a big recent move won more at nearly every lookback/threshold (e.g. 45min/1.0xATR: didn't-chase 34.5% WR/-$274.98 vs chased 40.8% WR/+$477.82). Zero candidates found where the "chasing" bucket was net negative.
Breakout persistence/smoothing — only label "breakout" for the first K consecutive bars of a fresh move, fall through to the (already-smoothed) EMA read after thatBarely moved the aggregate (K=1: 38.8% WR/+$289.07 vs baseline 38.6%/+$270.86; K≥3 converges to 38.3%/+$217.61). More fundamentally, checked against the three real losing trades directly: it wouldn't have stopped any of them — one bar had streak=3 and would reclassify to trending_up, but trend_follow treats trending_up and breakout-with-bullish-EMA identically (both fire BUY), so relabeling changes nothing; the other bar had streak=1, the minimum possible, "fresh" under any grace period.
Regime/ADX/ATR calculations independently verified — not the source of the problem Verified, not fixed

ATR and ADX are textbook Wilder's-method implementations, checked line by line — correct. trending_up/trending_down/ranging are already meaningfully smoothed (EMA(9)/EMA(21) on H1's own bars ≈ 9-21 hours of memory). breakout is the one exception in the whole regime system — a single-bar check (this bar made a new 20-bar high/low) that overrides the smoother EMA read, which is exactly why it kept re-firing all day during one continuous rally (H1 alternated trending_up/breakout nearly every hour from 01:00 onward — never a fresh signal by 16:10).

Decision — no change shipped

The unmodified, currently-live trend_follow logic nets +$202.84 to +$224.08 over the same ~9-week backtest window used for every candidate above, at a ~38% win rate — every attempt to beat it failed. Losing 3 in a row at a genuine 39% win rate has roughly a 22.6% chance by pure luck — not statistically alarming on its own, and not sufficient to outweigh a 342-trade backtest. breakout regime handling on trend_follow stays exactly as shipped in Part 2.

Part 7 — A different idea, tested and rejected
Grid trading — real 9-week backtest shows why not, on this instrument

Proposed as an alternative: instead of predicting direction, place a symmetric ladder of buy-the-dip / sell-the-rally orders around a reference price, closing each leg for one grid-spacing of profit. Backtested against this account's real 9-week XAUUSD history (56,054 M1 candles, July 2 – Sept 3) rather than synthetic data.

Over that window price rose from $4,111 to over $4,500 — a sustained ~9.5% trend, not a range. A grid's sell side bets price comes back down to close each leg; in a real trend it doesn't, so legs pile up, deeper underwater, indefinitely.

Every single spacing/cap combination tested shows the same shape: worst-case drawdown exceeds the realized gain, and legs are still open and underwater at the end of the window. Against this account's real balance (~$222), even the most conservative config tested would have meant a multi-times-over wipeout at its worst point.

Decision — not pursued

Grid trading's core assumption — mean reversion within a range — doesn't match this instrument's actual behavior over the tested period. Not implemented.
