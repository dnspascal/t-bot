First-pass threshold (4h/5pt) looked good in aggregate but didn't actually block the motivating trade Caught and corrected

Hand-verified the chosen threshold directly against today's real H1 ADX series before shipping: 4h/5pt's computed decline (3.92) fell just under its own 5pt cutoff — it would not have stopped the trades that motivated this fix. Re-checked every grid point against the real trades specifically (not just aggregate stats) and switched to 3h/3pt, which does block them, and is also the more robust choice — its own column (3pt→5pt→7pt) decreases smoothly as the threshold loosens, while 4h's column is non-monotonic (5pt scores worse than 7pt), a sign of overfitting to sampling noise rather than a real signal.

Part 2 — Close classification
"I watched them hit SL" — and the DB said unexplained_close

Directly reported: two ema_pullback XAUUSD SELLs closed $0.70 and $0.86 past their SL — a real stop-out during a fast reversal — but the DB recorded unexplained_close. Same root cause as the breakeven_sl classification bug fixed a few days ago, just not yet applied to sl_hit/tp_hit: a flat 5-pip ($0.50 on gold) symmetric tolerance around the exact level, too narrow to allow for real slippage past the trigger.

Decision — implemented and shipped

classifyBrokerClose's SL/TP checks are now direction-aware, same shape as the earlier breakeven fix: any overshoot in the "hit" direction counts unconditionally; only the near side keeps a small tolerance, for a close that triggers just shy of the exact level.

Checked against every historical unexplained_close on XAUUSD (4 trades) before shipping: all four reclassify correctly and consistently with their own net_profit sign — the two winners become tp_hit, the two losers (including the ones reported here) become sl_hit. None land ambiguous.

Part 3 — Peak-gain protection
A trade reached $39.40 of a $58.88 target and gave it all back to one cent — because nothing was watching between the two existing gates

Raised directly: several ema_pullback trades with far-away TPs got deep into profit, then rode all the way back to breakeven, because the existing protection design is two hard percentage-of-TP-distance gates with a dead zone between them:

  • Breakeven arms at 50% of TP distance, but only protects the entry price — none of the profit actually achieved.
  • peak_drawback requires 70% of TP distance before it protects anything at all. Below that, nothing.

Redesign: protect a fraction of peak gain, not a fixed finish line

Proposed replacing the two-stage cliff with a single continuous rule: once armed (same 50% arm point breakeven already uses), close if current gain falls to retentionPct of the trade's own peak gain — so the floor rises automatically as the trade makes new highs, rather than requiring one large fixed checkpoint regardless of how far the target actually is.

Backtested against 361 real XAUUSD trades (every strategy) over 60 days, replaying real M1 price data from each trade's actual open price/time — not just the ones that happened to live-fire this exact scenario:

25% is the best point in the grid, and sits in a real basin (20/25/30% cluster close together) rather than a single lucky value. Paired trade-by-trade against no protection at all: 56 trades helped, 43 hurt, 229 unaffected (never armed) — net +$154.55, a broad effect. Higher retention performs worse because it closes trades too early relative to genuine TP hits (106→4 as retention rises from 0→70%).

Honest limit: even at 25% retention the 60-day total is still net negative — this reduces give-back on trades that got into real profit, it doesn't fix why most strategies lose more often than they win.

Decision — implemented and shipped

peakGivebackPct replaces the old two-gate check as the active logic — closes once current gain falls to 25% of peak gain, armed at the same 50% point breakeven already uses. Breakeven itself is unchanged (still moves the real broker-side stop, a separate protection against the bot going offline). Per explicit instruction, the old peakDrawbackPct/peakDrawbackThreshold/peakDrawbackGatePct are kept in the code, unused, not deleted. decisionParams() updated to record peak_retention_arm_pct/peak_retention_pct.
