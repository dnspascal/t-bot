Root cause: the entry gate is a static yes/no with no concept of how long it's been true Fixed and shipped

Tested whether winners disproportionately enter on a fresh M5 regime alignment (few consecutive prior bars already showing the same regime) versus losers entering on a stale one (queries/regime_freshness_at_entry, 120-125 trades — XAUUSD trend_follow's entire live history in its current form, not just 30 days, since the strategy has been substantially rewritten multiple times this month). Clean, monotonic result:

1-2 bars (fresh)6453.1%$8.53
3-5 bars2128.6%$5.87
6+ bars (stale, avg 12.8 bars)3528.6%$4.69

Cumulative total P&L by streak cutoff peaks precisely at streak ≤ 2 bars ($197.64 from 68 trades) and degrades from streak=3 onward (adding bar-3 trades alone is -$8.76); it never recovers past that peak. The entry gate (H1 regime + M5 regime/EMA alignment + ADX≥20) fires the instant it becomes true but has no memory of when — the same static condition being true for the first time versus already true for an hour looks identical to it, even though one is a fresh move and the other is often already exhausted.

Counterfactual check before trusting the correlation: does rewinding a specific stale loser to its fresh moment turn it into a win? Mostly no — reframes the finding, doesn't invalidate it

Built a same-episode counterfactual (queries/counterfactual_fresh_entry): for every real stale losing trade, rewind to the exact bar its own regime run first became fresh, confirm the entry gate would have fired there too, and simulate forward from that bar using real M1 candles and the live SL/TP math. Of 39 gate-valid stale losers (full history): only 18.4% would have won entered fresh instead, 46.2% would still have lost, 35.9% unresolved within 24h. So this is not a timing-rescue effect — most stale losers were never going to be good trades even caught at their earliest moment. The real mechanism: episodes that only reach full alignment several bars late are a structurally weaker population of setups (marginal, indecisive moves) than episodes that align instantly (clean, strong moves) — freshness is a quality filter on which setups to bother with, not proof that late entries can be saved by better timing.

Decision — implemented and shipped

internal/strategy/trend_follow/strategy.go: added m5RegimeStreak tracking (increments while m5.Regime stays the same bar-to-bar, resets to 1 on change) and a new gate — hold if the streak exceeds maxFreshM5RegimeStreak = 2. Fails safe across restarts: unseenBarsTracked blocks entries for the first 2 bars actually observed after any process start, since a freshly-restarted tracker can't distinguish a regime that just started from one that's been running for an hour before the process came up. No DB-backed history recovery added (strategies currently have no DB access — trendfollow.New() takes no arguments) — the safe-default approach costs at most one missed trade per restart rather than risk a false "fresh" read. Deployed via feat → dev (squash) → main (merge) → tag.

Fix attempt 1 — widen the buffer to scale with ATR — rejected on the concept, not the numbers Tested, net-positive in isolation, rejected

Backtested queries/breakeven_buffer_sim: replacing the fixed 2-pip buffer with 0.3-0.6× M5 ATR at the real trigger moment. Net effect was strongly positive in aggregate (+$18 to +$33 across the 19 trades vs. the real +$1.41), but on inspection this doesn't fix the mechanism — it replaces it. A buffer wide enough to survive normal noise is, by the same construction, wide enough to give back real money on a genuine reversal: several trades that currently exit for pennies would instead exit for $1-8 real losses. That's no longer "no loss, no gain" — it's a smaller stop-loss with the break-even label still on it, which risks being confused with a genuine regime-driven loss (a functionally different, deliberately-larger exit). Rejected on that basis, not on the P&L number.

Fix attempt 2 — require an M1 candle close (not a wick touch) before triggering — tested, made things worse Empirically rejected

queries/breakeven_confirmation_sim: kept the exit price exactly as-is (the true fee-sized level, untouched) and only required an M1 candle to actually close past it, instead of firing on the first tick touch — the resting broker-side stop is what currently fires instantly, since a stop order can't be given a "wait and confirm" condition once placed. Result: zero of 19 trades flipped to a TP win, and the total went from +$1.41 to -$6.54. None of these were single-tick noise — every one had a real M1 candle close past the level before any later reversal, so confirmation doesn't filter anything out; it only delays the exit, letting price drift further past the tiny buffer before the (still fee-sized) close actually fires.

Decision — implemented and shipped

Left the buffer and the trigger mechanism untouched — raised when break-even engages instead. queries/breakeven_trigger_pct_sim: replayed the same 19 trades with the trigger point moved later (30-50% of TP progress instead of 25%), keeping the exact same fee-sized exit level. 25-40% changed nothing (all 19 still protected at the same tiny amount either way); at 50%, 3 of 19 reach TP instead, worth +$14.52 relative to the 25% baseline in this same replay, with every other trade still exiting at the same near-zero amount as before — no trade becomes a real loss at any tested percentage. internal/bot/watcher.go: breakEvenTriggerPct raised from 25.0 to 50.0. Not pushed further: the replay's fallback for "never reached this much of TP" isn't yet correct beyond 50% (it assumes the trade behaves the same as today, when in reality it would have gone completely unprotected for longer and could hit the original, much wider stop for a real loss instead) — flagged as the next step before testing anything past 50%, and worth stopping well short of peak_drawback_gate_pct = 70.0 regardless, to keep the two mechanisms doing distinct jobs.

Part 9 — The shared reversal watcher is only valid for trend-following strategies; three reversal strategies were left on it by mistake
ema_pullback, dd_oversold_bounce, and rsi_reversal enter deliberately against the exact signals the watcher checks — confirmed with real entry-time data, not just a duration statistic

Triggered by three live dd_oversold_bounce BUY trades today, each closed within 5.0 minutes by regime_against,rsi_against,ema_cross_against, all three winning (+$0.94, +$2.49, +$3.28) with price still climbing. Traced each one's real M1 forward path: all three would have hit full TP within 13-24 minutes of being force-closed (worth ~$13.30 each — 7-25% of that was actually captured). The M5 state at each close showed regime, RSI, and EMA all still reading exactly as they had at entry — nothing had changed, the watcher fired on the position's own entry conditions, not a real reversal.

Systemic, not isolated to one strategy — checked the same signature across all ten Root cause confirmed, fixed and shipped for 3 strategies

Fraction of each strategy's full history closed within one M5 candle by the watcher: ema_pullback 85.0% (17/20), dd_oversold_bounce 80.0% (8/10), rsi_reversal 75.0% (18/24) — versus dd_ranging_breakout and session_open at 0% (0/73, 0/20), and trend_follow/breakout at a moderate 45.1%/32.1%. sr_bounce and session_momentum already have UsesTrendWatcher() → false — someone had already recognized and fixed this exact mismatch for those two; these three were missed.

Confirmed with real entry-time market-state values, not just the duration statistic: 5 real ema_pullback SELL closes all show M5 EMA already crossed bullish and M5 RSI already above 50 at the exact moment of entry — because a pullback entry, by definition, requires a recent M5-level counter-move, and that counter-move necessarily drags M5's own lagging EMA/RSI into "against" territory before the trade even opens. 5 real rsi_reversal closes show the same for a different reason: H1 regime was breakout (not confirmed reversed — a real gap in the entry gate, which only excludes explicit TrendingUp) and M5 regime/RSI/EMA were all still reading the pre-reversal direction, since RSI hitting an extreme is definitionally ahead of the slower trend/EMA classifiers catching up.

The general principle, not three coincidences: the watcher's premise — "regime/RSI/EMA currently against my position is a warning sign" — only holds for trend-following strategies, where the position is supposed to already agree with those readings at entry. For a reversal strategy, the position is deliberately opposite those readings at entry; that's the entire bet. The check is true from the first candle by construction and never gets a chance to test whether the reversal thesis is actually failing.

Decision — implemented and shipped

UsesTrendWatcher() flipped to false for ema_pullback, dd_oversold_bounce, rsi_reversal — matching the precedent already set for sr_bounce/session_momentum. This does not leave them protected by the hard SL alone: UsesTrendWatcher only gates the regime/RSI/EMA/momentum block inside watchPositions (watcher.go:86) — break-even (checkBreakEven), peak-drawback, the 30-min/60-min time-stops, and the EOD close all run unconditionally for every position regardless of this flag, and are unchanged. Only the specific mechanism proven to fire on these strategies' own entry conditions was removed.
